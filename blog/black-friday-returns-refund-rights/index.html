Black Friday returns: what refund rights do you really have?

Black Friday is on 27 November this year, and Cyber Monday follows on 30 November. Returns rules differ sharply between the UK, EU and US, and "sale items are final" signs don't always mean what they say. Here is what the official guidance says, as of November 2026. United Kingdom: online purchases

If you buy online, by phone or by mail order, you can cancel within 14 days of receiving the item, even if nothing is wrong with it, according to GOV.UK. After cancelling you have a further 14 days to send the goods back, and the seller must refund you within 14 days of receiving them back. Standard delivery costs must be refunded too. If a seller never told you about this right, the cancellation period can run for up to 12 months.

There are exceptions to the 14-day rule, and the GOV.UK page doesn't say who pays return postage, so check the seller's terms before you buy. Our guide to refunds and returns covers the main exceptions.

United Kingdom: shops, faulty goods and sale items

In a physical shop there is no general legal right to return something just because you changed your mind, unless you bought it without seeing it. Many retailers offer a returns window anyway, but that is their own policy and it can have conditions. Faulty goods are different: if an item is faulty, not as described, or doesn't do what it should, you are entitled to a refund, and items returned within six months of purchase must normally be repaired or replaced unless the seller can prove the fault wasn't there when sold.

Sale items carry the same rights as full-price ones, and it is illegal to remove your rights or mislead you about them, for example with a blanket "no returns" sign. Learn more in our consumer rights guide.

European Union: the 14-day withdrawal right

EU shoppers who buy online or at a distance can withdraw within 14 days without giving a reason; for goods the period runs from delivery. Unlike the UK summary above, EU guidance says you pay the cost of returning goods after a withdrawal unless the seller offers to pay or failed to tell you about that cost before you bought. If goods are defective, the trader bears the return cost.

Exceptions include goods made to order or clearly personalised, sealed audio, video or software once unsealed, perishable goods, event and transport tickets, and digital content once you've started the download or streaming after agreeing to lose the right. Purchases from private individuals aren't covered, so check whether a marketplace seller is a business. Since June 2026 sellers must also offer an online withdrawal button during the withdrawal period.

United States: read the policy before you pay

The FTC's consumer guidance says online sellers must state whether you can return an item for a full refund, so the retailer's published policy is what counts. Before buying, check who pays return shipping, how many days you have, and whether there are restocking fees. Sellers often apply different policies to sale and clearance items, so a Black Friday purchase may be final sale or have a shorter window.

The FTC also says sellers must ship when promised, or within 30 days of receiving your order and payment if no time is promised. If you paid by credit card and never received the item, you can dispute the charge: see our guide to chargebacks.

Black Friday checklist: protect your refund

  • Screenshot the product page and returns policy on the day you order, and keep the confirmation email.
  • Check the seller. Fake shops multiply this week; see how to spot a fake online shop.
  • Pay by credit card or a protected method where you can, because it may give you a route to dispute a payment.
  • Unpack and test promptly, and keep packaging until the return window ends.
  • Send returns with proof of postage and note the date, so the refund clock is clear.
  • Escalate in writing if a seller refuses a valid refund, then follow our complaints steps.

Frequently asked questions

Can a UK shop refuse to refund a sale item?
If the item is faulty, not as described or not fit for purpose, no: sale items carry the same rights as full-price ones. If you simply changed your mind in a shop, there is no general legal right to a refund, so it depends on the shop's own policy.
How long do I have to return Black Friday purchases bought online in the UK?
GOV.UK says you can cancel up to 14 days after the item is delivered, then you have a further 14 days to return it. The seller must refund within 14 days of getting it back.
Do US shoppers have a legal right to return items?
The FTC guidance says sellers must disclose whether you can return an item for a full refund, so the retailer's published policy is what applies. Always read it before buying, especially for sale items.
Who pays for returns after a Black Friday order in the EU?
EU guidance says you pay the return cost after a withdrawal, unless the seller offered to pay or didn't tell you about the cost before you bought. For defective goods the trader pays.
