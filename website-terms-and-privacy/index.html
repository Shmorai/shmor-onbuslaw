Terms, privacy policy and cookies: what your website needs

A website that sells or collects any personal data needs a few legal pages, and a cookie banner that actually follows the rules. Here’s what’s required in the UK, EU and US.

What a privacy notice must cover

Under the GDPR and UK GDPR, tell people, in clear and plain language:

  • who you are and how to contact you (and your data protection officer, if you have one);
  • what personal data you collect, why, and your lawful basis for each purpose;
  • who you share it with, and whether it’s transferred abroad;
  • how long you keep it;
  • people’s rights, including access, correction, erasure and objection, and the right to complain to the regulator.

In the US, California’s CalOPPA requires commercial websites that collect personally identifiable information from California residents to post a privacy policy. Businesses covered by the California Consumer Privacy Act must also offer rights such as opting out of the “sale” or “sharing” of personal information, and honour Global Privacy Control signals.

Cookie consent done right

  • Strictly necessary cookies (such as a shopping basket or security) don’t need consent.
  • Other cookies, including most analytics, advertising and affiliate tracking, need consent before they are set.
  • Rejecting should be as easy as accepting, with no pre-ticked boxes and no “cookie walls” that leave no real choice.
  • Let people change their mind at any time.

Regulators such as the UK’s ICO and France’s CNIL actively enforce these rules. In the UK, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 creates new exemptions from consent for some low-risk cookies, such as certain analytics, as its provisions come into force, so follow the ICO’s latest guidance.

Terms and conditions

  • Write terms that fit your business and the law where your customers are. Copying another company’s terms can infringe their copyright and may not match your business or the right law.
  • In the UK and EU, unfair terms in consumer contracts aren’t binding on consumers, and terms must be transparent.
  • Cover ordering, prices, delivery, cancellation and returns, faulty goods, liability and how to complain.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a privacy policy generator?
Generators can be a useful starting point, but the notice must accurately describe what your site actually does, including every tool and plugin that collects data. Review it whenever you add new tools.
Do I need a cookie banner if I only use analytics?
In the EU, generally yes, unless the analytics tool is set up to qualify for a national exemption. In the UK, some analytics may become exempt under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, subject to conditions; check current ICO guidance.
Does a small US website need a privacy policy?
If it collects personal information from California residents, CalOPPA requires one, and most app stores, ad networks and payment providers require one too. In practice, almost every commercial site should have one.
